# 0008 · The name: kleos

Date: 2026-11-07

## Decision

The project is called **kleos**.

## Why

κλέος (_kléos_, said "KLEH-os") is the Homeric word for the glory a person earns through deeds. It comes from the verb _to hear_: kleos is literally "what is heard" about you, and it lives in what other people say, not in what you claim. Achilles chose a short life with _kléos áphthiton_, undying glory, over a long life without it.

That's what these cards are. They don't total up your own repositories; they show the work you did in other people's projects, the part of your reputation that lives in someone else's code, issues and discussions. The name also stays true if the project grows beyond cards: a page, a JSON feed, a CLI.

It's five letters, easy to say, has no visible seam, and is free under the owner's account. The largest same-name repositories are small personal projects (about 20 stars).

## Rejected

- **`tributary`**, the first proposal. A tributary is a stream feeding a larger river, but the word also means a state paying tribute to a stronger one, and it read as "paying taxes".
- **`gh-stats`**, the working title. It's generic, nine GitHub CLI extensions already use it, and it says nothing about the angle: other people's projects rather than personal totals.
- **`gh-footprint`** and **`gh-reach`**. They're free and descriptive, but forgettable, and the `gh-` prefix commits the project to being a GitHub CLI extension.
- **Blends such as `gitseki` and `gitora`.** The seam between "git" and the rest is visible. "Seki" alone doesn't read as "trace" in Japanese, and "gitora" has no meaning.
- **`kiseki`** (軌跡, "trajectory", the word behind Falcom's _Trails_ games) was the runner-up. It's elegant, but without kanji it's usually heard as 奇跡, "miracle".
- **`lugh`**, from the Irish god who was admitted at Tara because he was master of every craft. It's a great story, but most people can't tell it's said "loo".
- **Other myths** taken by large projects or hard to say: `nuwa` (Microsoft), `ichnaea` (Mozilla), `muninn` (its twin Huginn is a large project), `ariadne`, `ogham`, `chitragupta`.
